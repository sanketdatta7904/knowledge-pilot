Chapter 5: Linear Functions and Slope

5.1 What is Slope?

When we walk up a hill, we experience steepness. In mathematics, we measure this steepness precisely using the concept of slope. The slope of a line tells us how steep the line is and in which direction it goes.

Definition: The slope m of a line is the ratio of the vertical change (rise) to the horizontal change (run) between any two points on the line.

    m = Δy / Δx = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)

The slope is a ratio — it compares two quantities. A slope of 2/3 means: for every 3 units we move to the right, the line goes up by 2 units.

5.2 The Slope Triangle

To read the slope from a graph, we use a slope triangle. A slope triangle is a right triangle drawn between two points on a line, where:
- The horizontal leg shows the run (Δx)
- The vertical leg shows the rise (Δy)

Steps to construct a slope triangle:
1. Choose two points on the line that lie on grid intersections.
2. From the left point, draw a horizontal line segment to the right.
3. From the end of that segment, draw a vertical segment up (or down) to the second point.
4. Read off the lengths of both segments.

When the slope is positive (m > 0):
- The line goes upward from left to right.
- The vertical segment of the slope triangle points UP.
- Example: A line through (0,0) and (3,2) has slope m = 2/3 > 0.

When the slope is negative (m < 0):
- The line goes downward from left to right.
- The vertical segment of the slope triangle points DOWN.
- Example: A line through (0,4) and (2,0) has slope m = -4/2 = -2 < 0.

Special cases:
- Horizontal lines have slope m = 0 (no vertical change).
- Vertical lines have undefined slope (division by zero).

5.3 The Equation y = mx (Proportional Lines)

A line through the origin can be described by the equation y = mx, where m is the slope.

Properties:
- The line always passes through (0, 0).
- For any point (x, y) on the line: y/x = m (constant ratio).
- This represents a proportional relationship between x and y.

To determine the equation from a graph:
1. Construct a slope triangle.
2. Calculate m = rise/run.
3. Write y = mx.

Examples:
- If m = 2: y = 2x. For every unit increase in x, y increases by 2.
- If m = -1/2: y = -½x. The line falls, losing half a unit of y for each unit of x.

5.4 The y-Intercept and the Equation y = mx + b

Not all lines pass through the origin. The general equation of a line is:

    y = mx + b

where:
- m is the slope (steepness and direction)
- b is the y-intercept (where the line crosses the y-axis)

Reading the y-intercept from a graph:
- Find where the line crosses the y-axis (x = 0).
- The y-coordinate at that point is b.

When b > 0: The line crosses the y-axis above the origin.
When b < 0: The line crosses the y-axis below the origin.
When b = 0: The line passes through the origin (reduces to y = mx).

To determine the full equation from a graph:
1. Read the y-intercept b (where the line crosses the y-axis).
2. Construct a slope triangle and calculate m.
3. Write y = mx + b.

5.5 Plotting a Line from its Equation

Given an equation y = mx + b, we can plot the line:

Method 1 (using slope and intercept):
1. Mark the y-intercept (0, b) on the y-axis.
2. From that point, use the slope to find a second point:
   - Move 1 unit right (or the denominator of m).
   - Move m units up (or the numerator of m; down if negative).
3. Draw a straight line through both points.

Method 2 (using a table of values):
1. Choose 2-3 x-values.
2. Calculate corresponding y-values.
3. Plot the points and connect them.

5.6 Slope as Rate of Change

In real-world contexts, slope represents a rate of change:
- A water tank filling at 3 liters per minute: m = 3 (liters/minute)
- A car traveling at 60 km/h: distance = 60t, so m = 60 (km/hour)
- A candle burning down at 2 cm per hour: m = -2 (cm/hour, negative because height decreases)

The slope tells us:
- HOW FAST something changes (absolute value of m)
- IN WHICH DIRECTION it changes (sign of m)
- The UNITS of the rate (units of y per unit of x)

Growth processes (m > 0): The quantity increases over time.
Decay processes (m < 0): The quantity decreases over time.
Constant processes (m = 0): No change over time.

5.7 Slope as a Multiplicative Factor

The slope can also be understood as a scaling or multiplicative factor:
- If y = 3x, then tripling x triples y. The slope 3 acts as a multiplier.
- Enlargements: A photocopier set to 150% enlarges by factor 1.5 (slope of mapping).
- Unit conversions: Converting km to miles uses the factor 0.621 (slope of conversion line).

In proportional relationships (y = mx):
- m tells us the factor by which x is multiplied to get y.
- Doubling m doubles the output for any given input.
- The slope IS the constant of proportionality.

Summary:
The slope of a line is a versatile concept that can be understood as:
1. A ratio of two changes (rise over run)
2. A rate of change in applied contexts
3. A multiplicative factor in proportional relationships

Each interpretation offers different insight, and fluency with all three is essential for working with linear functions.
